# Outreach — Microsoft Partner bench overflow subcontracting

**Goal**: Establish 1–3 subcontracting relationships with Microsoft delivery partners where Althor can pick up overflow work in AI / Copilot / Azure architecture. Parallel track to the platform pivot — doesn't compete with Upwork/Toptal/Codementor.

**Why this works**: Microsoft delivery partners (Avanade, Slalom, Quisitive, etc.) constantly have bench gaps when a project's AI/agent component lands outside their senior staff's expertise. They prefer subcontracting a known specialist over recruiting a new FTE. Margins are lower than direct ($100–150/hr to you, $200–300/hr to the partner) but the work is pre-sold, pre-scoped, and consistent.

## Follow-up cadence

- **Day 0**: Send initial email
- **Day 5**: One follow-up if no response — single line: "Bumping this in case it landed in spam — {{ORIGINAL_THREAD}}"
- **Day 14**: Final follow-up — "No bandwidth right now? Happy to circle back in 90 days, just let me know."
- **Day 90**: Re-engage with anyone who didn't respond OR who said "not now" — short note tied to a fresh case study or essay

---

## What to lead with after they respond

The 20-minute intro call has one goal: get them to flag you on the next AI / Copilot opportunity that exceeds their bench. Don't pitch a specific engagement. Don't try to close on the first call. Send rate sheet + abstracted client list afterward.

### Materials to send post-call:
1. Rate sheet (subcontracted rates: $100/hr blended, $120/hr architecture-only, $140/hr emergency-call)
2. Abstracted client list (de-identified case studies — same as on althor.dev)
3. Sample MSA / SOW template (their preferred shape works fine; flag if you don't carry E&O insurance independently and rely on partner coverage)
4. One sample deliverable (an architecture review redacted) — proves the work quality without spilling a real client engagement

---

## Why this isn't sleazy

Microsoft partners openly subcontract to specialists when bench gaps appear. It's a known economic model. The pitch is honest — Althor is a specialist who can land in a project for 4–12 weeks without the partner needing to hire. They keep the client relationship; Althor takes the technical scope. Everyone wins except the FTE who didn't get hired (which would have been a bad fit at 4 weeks of work anyway).
